Le bloc qui verrouille ton visage sur une photo

Ce plan n'a pas été tourné. Il a été écrit — et le voici en entier, sans rien couper.

Pourquoi ce prompt tient, alors que les autres dérivent

La plupart des prompts vidéo décrivent une scène. Celui-ci décrit d'abord une identité, et il la répète à chaque seconde où elle doit tenir. C'est ce bloc qui empêche le visage de se transformer en cours de plan :

[CHARACTER LOCK]
<<<image_1>>> — DRIVER. Identity, face, skin tone, hairstyle, hair color,
facial structure, build and clothing are locked exactly to the reference image
and must not drift, blend, morph or be reinterpreted at any point.
[…] Same person, same clothes, same hair from 0s to 15s.

Trois choses le font marcher, et elles sont toutes copiables :

  • Un jeton, pas un nom. <<<image_1>>> renvoie à l'image jointe. Écrire « un homme brun de 35 ans » laisse le modèle inventer ; renvoyer à une photo ne lui laisse rien à inventer.
  • La borne temporelle. « from 0s to 15s » — sans elle, le verrouillage tient les deux premières secondes puis lâche.
  • La liste des dérives interdites. drift, blend, morph, reinterpret : ce sont les quatre façons dont un modèle contourne une consigne d'identité.

La structure, en six blocs

L'ordre compte. Le modèle lit du général au particulier, et chaque bloc contraint le suivant.

  1. [TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS] — durée, format, rendu. « Single continuous take, no cuts » vaut mieux que dix mots sur le style.
  2. [CORE CONCEPT] — l'idée en un paragraphe. Ce que le spectateur doit comprendre, pas ce qu'il doit voir.
  3. [CHARACTER LOCK] — l'identité, verrouillée. Le bloc ci-dessus.
  4. [ENVIRONMENT] — le décor, et surtout ce qui ne doit pas bouger.
  5. [CAMERA] — un opérateur, une caméra, un mouvement justifié.
  6. [CHRONOLOGICAL TIMELINE] — seconde par seconde. C'est le bloc le plus long, et c'est lui qui fait la différence entre un plan et une soupe.
Ce qui compte le plus

La timeline seconde par seconde. Un modèle vidéo ne « comprend » pas une histoire : il remplit une durée. Si tu ne lui dis pas ce qui se passe à 3,4 s, il l'invente — et c'est là que le visage part.

Le prompt entier

Tel qu'il a été envoyé. Rien n'est coupé, rien n'est reformulé. Joins ta photo de référence, et remplace la scène par la tienne en gardant les six blocs.

[TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS]
Duration: 15 seconds. Aspect ratio 9:16 vertical. Single continuous handheld smartphone take — no cuts, no transitions, no hidden edits. Raw amateur iPhone capture: auto-exposure, auto-focus, auto white balance, all visibly hunting and failing in real time. No color grading, no LUT, no styled lighting, no stabilization rig. 30fps native motion feel with rolling-shutter skew on fast pans and jello-wobble on impacts. Photorealistic. Diegetic sound only — absolutely no music, no score, no sound bed.

[CORE CONCEPT]
A bystander films a matte black low-slung mid-engine V10 supercar rolling slowly down a sun-bleached Los Angeles street. The window is down. A young woman on the sidewalk shouts a question at the driver. The driver answers with total calm — and then, one beat later, breaks the rear end loose in a controlled drift and disappears, leaving the operator physically unable to keep up and the woman screaming in shock. The humor and the flex come from the contrast between the mundane answer and the violence of the departure.

[CHARACTER LOCK]
<<<image_1>>> — DRIVER. Identity, face, skin tone, hairstyle, hair color, facial structure, build and clothing are locked exactly to the reference image and must not drift, blend, morph or be reinterpreted at any point. Athletic build, immaculately tailored outfit, every detail crisp against the unpolished street context. Expression baseline: composed, unhurried, a barely-there smirk at one corner of the mouth, steely forward gaze. Left arm resting on the door sill in the early beats. Same person, same clothes, same hair from 0s to 15s.

WOMAN — young woman, early twenties, casual streetwear, hair loose to the shoulders, small crossbody bag. Appears at the left edge of frame, partially turned toward the car, face visible in three-quarter profile when she speaks. Expressive, animated. Locked wardrobe, hair and build for the entire take.

PASSING PEDESTRIAN — adult in a dark jacket, seen only as an anonymous back crossing frame. No face.

[ENVIRONMENT]
Wide Los Angeles street, mid-afternoon, harsh overhead sun almost directly above. Pale grey asphalt with heat shimmer, faded lane paint, oil stains, cracked patch repairs. Sidewalk with concrete seams and scattered litter. Chain-link fence roughly twenty feet behind the car, low commercial buildings, utility poles and sagging power lines, a few dusty palms. Parked cars along the far curb. Loose crowd of pedestrians. Air is dry and hazy. Environment stays geometrically consistent the entire take — buildings, fence, parked cars and pole positions never morph, shift or regenerate as the camera pans.

[CAMERA]
Handheld vertical phone held at chest-to-eye height by an untrained operator reacting live, not composing. Wide phone lens with mild barrel distortion and edge softness. Framing is imperfect throughout: heads clipped, subjects off-center, tilt never fully level. Micro-shake constant but not seizure-like. Focus hunts aggressively and repeatedly, locking onto the wrong plane before snapping back. Auto-exposure visibly lurches when the frame moves between sun and shade. Chromatic aberration bleeds purple and green along high-contrast edges. All reframing is physically motivated by the operator chasing the car — never a cut, never a perspective jump.

[CHRONOLOGICAL TIMELINE]
0.0–1.0s — Frame already unsteady, pointed loosely down the street, over-exposed on bright asphalt. Nothing has happened yet. Ambient traffic, distant crowd murmur, wind buffeting the mic with low crackle.

1.0–2.0s — A deep authoritative low-frequency rumble rolls in from offscreen left. The camera swings fast toward it, momentarily clipping the top of the frame, catching a blurred pedestrian shoulder in the near foreground. Rolling-shutter skew leans the vertical poles during the swing.

2.0–3.0s — The matte black supercar slides into frame from the left, moving slowly. The rumble thickens into a resonant growl that vibrates the audio channel and pushes the phone mic into slight low-end distortion. Auto-focus hunts hard: the car body goes soft, the background sharpens for roughly half a second, then snaps back onto the low roofline.

3.0–4.0s — The driver's window is fully down. <<<image_1>>> is visible from the chest up, sharply lit by hard overhead sun, a dense shadow under the jaw, no fill, unflattering in the best paparazzi sense. Calm, composed, faint smirk, eyes forward.

4.0–5.5s — The WOMAN steps into the left edge of frame, half-obscuring the front bumper, turns three-quarters toward the open window and calls out loudly over the street noise, voice raw and unmixed, one hand half-raised. Auto-focus briefly abandons <<<image_1>>>'s face and locks onto her shoulder before hunting back.

5.5–6.5s — <<<image_1>>> turns their head slightly toward the window. The smirk deepens by a fraction. Posture stays relaxed and unhurried against the car's slow rolling momentum. A short deliberate pause — the beat before the answer is allowed to breathe.

6.5–8.0s — In a crystal-clear, confidently projected, unmistakably native French voice that cuts cleanly above the engine rumble, <<<image_1>>> delivers the answer. Casual precision, no affectation, no performance.

8.0–9.0s — The WOMAN's mouth opens slightly, eyebrows lifting — registering the answer. The operator exhales audibly into the mic, a half-laugh of surprise. The frame dips, catching the rear quarter panel and the spinning rim, spokes strobing in the harsh sun. Lens flare clips the upper right corner as an uncorrected streak of blown yellow-white.

9.0–10.0s — A PEDESTRIAN walks fully through frame between camera and car, blocking the shot for nearly a full second. The operator does not stop recording — the frame is simply obscured by the back of a jacket, then clears.

10.0–11.0s — The car is three-quarters past, rear wing visible. The operator has tracked into shade; exposure darkens, struggles, then lurches brighter. Chromatic aberration fringes the bodywork against the pale sky.

11.0–11.6s — <<<image_1>>>'s forearm withdraws from the door sill. The window glass rises smoothly and seals. The silhouette settles square in the driver's seat, hands returning to the wheel. The engine note drops for a fraction of a second as the car settles — an audible loading of tension.

11.6–12.1s — Brake lights flick off. The rear of the car visibly squats, the front end lifting a few centimetres as weight transfers rearward. Suspension compresses. Loose grit and dust lift off the asphalt behind the diffuser. A held half-beat of near-silence under the rising intake.

12.1–13.6s — FULL THROTTLE, REAR END BREAKS LOOSE. The V10 climbs into a hard flat-out wail, exhausts cracking on the upshift. The rear tyres lose traction and spin up, the tail stepping out to the right in a controlled power slide. The driver applies visible opposite lock, front wheels counter-steering, the car holding a clean angle of roughly twenty degrees as it slides forward down its own lane — never crossing into oncoming traffic, never touching anything. Thick white tyre smoke pours from the rear arches and drifts across the frame, momentarily washing out the background. The car then hooks up, straightens, and pulls away with brutal, physically believable velocity, shrinking in the frame as the gap opens. The WOMAN recoils half a step back, both hands flying up, and SCREAMS — a raw, unfiltered, genuinely startled shriek, mouth wide, eyes wide, hair blown backward by the displaced air. The camera whips right to chase and cannot keep up: the pan lags behind the car, the subject drifts to the right edge and then out of frame entirely for a fraction of a second before the operator overcorrects. Rolling-shutter skew leans the entire frame. Motion blur smears the background into horizontal streaks. Auto-exposure blows out as the camera swings into direct sun. The mic clips hard on the exhaust peaks and on the scream, and briefly gates.

13.6–14.6s — The operator recovers, frame settling raggedly on the receding car, now small and distorted by heat-haze down the centre of the road, a hanging cloud of tyre smoke drifting laterally across the lane behind it. The engine wail dopplers downward as distance opens. The WOMAN is still half-turned away, one hand over her mouth, laughing and shouting something unintelligible. Nearby pedestrians turn their heads.

À réadapter, forcément

Ce prompt a été écrit pour cette scène, avec cette photo. Recopié tel quel, il te donnera cette voiture et cette rue. Les blocs sont réutilisables ; leur contenu, non. Attends-toi à deux ou trois passes avant que ça tombe juste — et si le visage dérive encore, c'est presque toujours la borne temporelle du CHARACTER LOCK qui manque.
